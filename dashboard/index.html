How the board is built

The board answers one question: which pre-token protocol is worth farming next, and what do I do about it? A protocol's rank is its farmable expected value: the fitted launch value, times the token odds from its peers' record, times a farmability gate of 1 or 0. The gate is the point. A board ranked by size alone puts exchanges and bridges on top, and none of those can be farmed for a new launch. This board puts them at the bottom, with the reason shown, and ranks the farmable protocols first.

1. The fitted value

The fitted value is a ridge regression of log market cap on the snapshot's own figures, fitted at runtime on the traded protocols (the comps): protocols with a recorded market cap, excluding exchanges. The regression is fit in log space because market caps span orders of magnitude. The fitted value is what the protocol's token would be worth if it launched and traded like its peers — no more, no less.

2. Every term is tested twice, or it is dropped

Six candidate terms were tested: log TVL, its square, seven-day growth, log age, log chain count, and category dummies. Each term faced two blind tests — leave-one-out (predict each traded protocol without itself) and a walk-forward split (fit on the older half of traded protocols, predict the newer half). A term survives only when removing it makes both tests worse. The table below prints both tests' numbers per term, from the model's own run. Terms that failed were dropped, not hidden.

3. Token odds, from the peers' record

A pre-token protocol may never issue a token, so the fitted value is multiplied by its token odds: the share of mature peers in its own category — protocols listed at least a year before the snapshot — that record a token, Laplace-smoothed toward the global mature rate. A category with fewer than three mature peers takes the global rate. The snapshot records no token-issue dates, so no age adjustment is fitted; that is a stated limit, not a hidden one.

5. Uncertainty, priced per kind

The interval on each row is a conformal interval: the 80% quantile of the leave-one-out residuals, per category where the category has at least eight traded protocols, else the pooled quantile. It is an honest out-of-sample interval — each traded protocol predicted without itself — not a band drawn around the fit. Market caps vary enormously at the same TVL, so some categories' intervals span a factor of sixty; that is what the data says.

6. Verdicts

FARM means the farmable expected value is at least $10,000,000 — a launch big enough to be worth real capital. THIN means $3,000,000 to $10,000,000 — real but small. SKIP means below $3,000,000, or not farmable at all. The thresholds are set from this snapshot's own distribution of expected values, and the counts are printed with the board.

Limits, stated plainly

Market cap is only weakly predictable from these figures: the fitted model explains a small share of the variance, and its typical error is most of the value itself. The board is therefore a ranking by the best available estimate, not a promise. Token odds are a peer rate, not a fitted probability. The snapshot is one cross-section from one source; a protocol it records as pre-token may still have a token its record does not carry. Nothing here predicts whether or when a token is issued — only what it would be worth if it launched, and whether farming it is even possible.

Live tokens — the market's own values

These protocols already trade, so the board shows the market's own market cap for them, never a model's. They are kept apart from the ranked board: a launch radar ranks what has not launched yet.
